# Task 2 — SN-38 / BRAF interaction novelty

_Date: 2026-04-25 · Author: Seungho Cook · PubMed queries F, G, H, T2a–T2d (52 unique records)._

## The honest question

> "Is PRISM topotecan/irinotecan ΔLFC selectivity for BRAF-like thyroid lines a genuinely novel pharmacological finding, or is it already implicit in the well-established BRAF-mCRC + irinotecan literature?"

## Three concentric circles of prior art

### Circle 1 — Established: BRAF-mutant patients receive irinotecan-containing chemo backbones (clinical, not biology)

20 hits in **T2a (irinotecan + BRAF + colorectal)**. Representative records:

- **PMID 41852303** (2026, *Cancer Medicine*) — Encorafenib + cetuximab vs irinotecan/cetuximab or FOLFIRI/cetuximab in BRAF V600E mCRC.
- **PMID 41505697** (2026, *J Clin Oncol*, TRIPLETE) — FOLFOXIRI + panitumumab as upfront for RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC.
- **PMID 41980919** (2026, *Sig Transduct Target Ther*, STRATEGIC-1) — strategy trial; uses irinotecan as backbone.

These trials use irinotecan as a **standard-of-care chemotherapy backbone** in mCRC; they do **not** claim that BRAF mutation confers irinotecan/SN-38 *sensitivity*. The BEACON regimen (encorafenib + cetuximab) was specifically designed as a BRAF-targeted alternative to chemo, suggesting the field treats BRAF-mut mCRC as a **chemo-resistant** group, not a chemo-sensitive one.

**Conclusion (circle 1):** clinical exposure is shared, but the *causal* claim "BRAF mutation ↔ topo-I sensitivity" is **not** established in CRC clinical literature.

### Circle 2 — Adjacent: TOP1 ↔ BRAF axis in melanoma resistance (mechanism, but wrong tumour type and wrong direction)

- **PMID 34560251** (2021, *Pharmacological Research*) — "TOP1 modulation during melanoma progression and in adaptative resistance to BRAF and MEK inhibitors." TOP1 expression dynamics under BRAF-i pressure.
- **PMID 36559089** (2022, *Pharmaceutics*) — Encorafenib (BRAFi) inhibits ABCC1 transporter, sensitising to certain chemo (PK-level interaction).
- **PMID 36693125** (2023, *Cancer Discovery*) — Antibody-Exatecan conjugates (ADC class with TOP1 payloads, not BRAF-stratified).

**Conclusion (circle 2):** A TOP1-BRAF mechanistic link exists in **melanoma resistance** literature. It is **not** the same claim as "BRAF-like thyroid cell lines are SN-38 sensitive" — the resistance vs primary-sensitivity directions differ, and the tumour types differ.

### Circle 3 — Closest neighbour: PRISM/DepMap + BRAF stratification of drug response

- **T2c (SN-38 + DepMap):** 1 hit — unrelated CRC HIPEC paper (PMID 41634518, 2026).
- **T2d (PRISM + BRAF + drug sensitivity):** 4 hits — none on thyroid, none on SN-38.
  - PMID 39835134 — melanoma immunopeptidome (BRAF/MEK-i resistance).
  - PMID 35116291 — TRIM51 biomarker in melanoma.
  - PMID 35066105 — osimertinib resistance in NSCLC.
  - PMID 21768778 — hyperspectral imaging in melanoma (not the same PRISM).

**Conclusion (circle 3):** PubMed-indexed literature has **no record** of a thyroid-PRISM analysis stratified by BRAF subtype examining SN-38 / topo-I selectivity. To the best of this query's coverage, our v14 finding is a first specific report.

## Verdict

The PRISM SN-38 / topotecan / irinotecan BRAF-selectivity in thyroid is:

- **Conceptually adjacent** to (i) BRAF-mCRC clinical chemo exposure and (ii) melanoma TOP1-BRAF resistance literature.
- **Not implied** by either of the above. The CRC literature treats BRAF-mut as chemo-*resistant*; the melanoma literature treats TOP1 as a *resistance* dynamics gene under BRAF-i.
- **First thyroid-specific PRISM read-out** of this kind in the queried PubMed coverage.

Frame v14 §5.Y.4 as: *"While BRAF-mutant patients receive topo-I poisons clinically (e.g., FOLFIRINOX in CRC), no published cell-line pharmacogenomic screen has previously reported a primary BRAF-status–dependent sensitivity to SN-38-class agents in thyroid. We provide the first such observation, with the explicit caveat that PRISM cannot disentangle TROP2-directed delivery from cell-intrinsic SN-38 vulnerability — this is testable by free-topotecan vs sacituzumab govitecan xenograft comparison."*
