## Nature Reviews Clinical Oncology

Subject: Presubmission enquiry: Review on vaccine-ready neoantigen prioritization

Dear Editors,

I am writing to ask whether Nature Reviews Clinical Oncology would be interested in a Review or Perspective provisionally titled "From neoantigen discovery to vaccine-ready prioritization."

Personalized neoantigen vaccines are again clinically prominent, with encouraging signals across melanoma, pancreatic cancer and renal cell carcinoma. However, the computational bottleneck has shifted from identifying HLA binders to prioritizing a small number of manufacturable vaccine candidates under severe class imbalance, incomplete negative labels, source shift and public benchmark overlap. We propose a clinically oriented Review that synthesizes current predictor families and argues for a practical evaluation contract based on contamination-controlled splits, AUPRC, top-k precision, enrichment over prevalence, calibration and OOD-aware abstention.

The article would not be a catalogue of algorithms or a presentation of unpublished data. Instead, it would focus on the translational decision layer connecting neoantigen prediction to vaccine manufacture and immune testing.

I attach a short synopsis and proposed display-item plan. I would be grateful for your advice on whether this topic would be of interest to the journal.

Sincerely,

Seungho Cook

## Nature Cancer

Subject: Presubmission enquiry: Perspective on vaccine-ready benchmarks for neoantigen prediction

Dear Editors,

I am writing to ask whether Nature Cancer would consider a Perspective provisionally titled "Neoantigen prediction needs vaccine-ready benchmarks, not another AUROC leaderboard."

The central argument is that the field has built increasingly powerful neoantigen predictors, but evaluation has not kept pace with deployment. For personalized vaccines, the clinically relevant question is whether a method can prioritize the handful of candidates worth manufacturing or testing for a new patient without relying on public-corpus overlap, HLA shortcuts or source-specific label bias. We propose to reframe neoantigen prediction as a top-k, source-aware, contamination-controlled ranking problem under label uncertainty.

The article would be forward-looking and balanced: public predictors are valuable, but benchmark design and reporting standards now determine whether their performance translates to vaccine decision-making. We would propose a practical evaluation contract covering exact and near overlap, HLA and source holdouts, AUPRC/top-k/enrichment, calibration and OOD-aware abstention.

I attach a one-page synopsis for your consideration.

Sincerely,

Seungho Cook

Dear Editors,

I would like to propose a Review or Perspective for Trends in Cancer provisionally titled "The new bottleneck in personalized cancer vaccines: ranking the manufacturable neoantigens."

The article would synthesize recent clinical progress in personalized neoantigen vaccines and recent computational progress in neoantigen prediction, then focus on the under-discussed bottleneck between the two: practical candidate prioritization. We would argue that vaccine pipelines require top-k, source-aware and contamination-controlled ranking metrics rather than AUROC-centric leaderboards.

The piece would be concise and figure-driven, with display items on the vaccine prioritization funnel, predictor taxonomy, benchmark failure modes and a practical evaluation checklist.

I would be grateful to know whether this topic would fit the journal's current interests.

## JITC

Subject: Review proposal: practical evaluation standards for neoantigen vaccine prioritization

Dear Editors,

I am preparing a Review on computational prioritization for personalized neoantigen vaccines and would like to ask whether it would be suitable for the Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer.

The manuscript would focus on a practical problem for tumor immunology and cancer immunotherapy: how to evaluate neoantigen rankers when the clinically relevant output is a small candidate list for manufacture or immune testing. We would cover current predictor families, public benchmark limitations, source and assay shift, incomplete negative labels, top-k utility, calibration and abstention. The goal is to provide a practical evaluation contract for future neoantigen prediction studies and vaccine-design pipelines.

The article would be balanced and would not present unpublished model results as central evidence.

Sincerely,

Seungho Cook
