# PTPRE (sonnet)

**BIOLOGY:** PTPRE (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Receptor Type E) is a transmembrane phosphatase that regulates cellular signaling by dephosphorylating tyrosine residues on target proteins. In thyroid carcinoma, PTPRE functions as an oncogene by activating both AKT and ERK1/2 signaling pathways, which are downstream effectors in the MAPK cascade commonly dysregulated in BRAF-like thyroid cancers. This dual pathway activation suggests PTPRE may serve as a convergence point linking growth factor signaling to proliferative and survival pathways characteristic of aggressive thyroid malignancies.

**DRUGGABILITY:** Direct pharmacological targeting of PTPRE remains limited, with no specific small molecule inhibitors currently in clinical development for this phosphatase. However, studies in hepatocellular carcinoma demonstrate that PTPRE inhibition can improve response to sorafenib, a multi-kinase inhibitor already approved for thyroid cancer treatment. This suggests potential for combination therapeutic strategies using existing tyrosine kinase inhibitors alongside experimental PTPRE-targeting approaches, though specific PTPRE inhibitors remain in early research phases.

**CLINICAL:** Direct clinical trial evidence for PTPRE targeting in thyroid cancer is currently absent. While sorafenib combination potential has been demonstrated in hepatocellular carcinoma models, no thyroid cancer-specific clinical studies have been reported for PTPRE modulation.

**RESEARCH GAP:** The most valuable next experiment would be functional validation of PTPRE inhibition in BRAF-like versus RAS-like thyroid cancer cell lines to determine subtype-specific therapeutic sensitivity and optimal combination partners with existing targeted therapies.
