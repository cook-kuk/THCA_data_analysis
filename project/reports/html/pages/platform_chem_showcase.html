Platform · Therapeutic Chemistry

Chemical space, compressed by AI that never tires.

A transparent, reproducible pipeline collapses 51,711 gene measurements into 2,773 replication-validated public biomarkers, then funnels the top eight into structural, literature-anchored compound cohorts. Every step is inspectable, every call is traceable, and every claim is scoped to computational triage.

Neural network pipeline

Three models, stacked end-to-end, take raw expression through replication and into druggability.

Every target that surfaces on this page has passed all three stages: a cohort-scale differential-expression screen, an independent replication check in held-out public datasets, and a chemical-matter filter that cross-references ChEMBL bioactivities with the peer-reviewed thyroid-cancer literature. Upstream model weights, thresholds, and intermediate tables are published alongside this dashboard.

Model 01 · Discovery
Differential expression

A Welch t-test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction scans 51,711 transcripts across the TCGA-THCA cohort for tumor-versus-normal separation, yielding a ranked candidate pool with explicit false-discovery-rate budgets.

Input · 51,711 genes  |  Output · FDR-adjusted ranking
Model 02 · Replication
Independent cohort check

Candidate biomarkers are re-tested in two independent GEO cohorts (GSE27155, GSE126698). Only genes that retain the same effect direction at p < 0.05 in both progress — 2,773 pass the bar.

Surviving · 2,773 replicated biomarkers
Model 03 · Druggability
Chemistry + literature anchor

Each replicated biomarker is queried against ChEMBL for chemical matter at pChEMBL ≥ 6, then cross-referenced against thyroid-indexed PubMed abstracts and available structural coverage. Eight targets pass; fifteen compounds surface.

Interactive · 3D binding

Structural context for every shortlisted target.

Experimental co-crystal structures are preferred where available; apo crystal structures fill the gap next; AlphaFold predictions cover the remainder. The top ChEMBL hit for each target is overlaid as a 3D ball-and-stick ligand, and residues within 5 Å of any bound ligand atom are labelled as the reference binding pocket. All structures and ligand coordinates are pre-computed and served statically — no external API calls at load time.

    Potency snapshot

    Top-3 compound pChEMBL ranges for the four targets with chemical matter.

    For each target with one or more ChEMBL hits, the bar spans the potency range of the top three public compounds; the amber diamond marks the single best reported pChEMBL. Values are pooled from heterogeneous public assays (binding, cell-based, functional) and should be read as a relative signal rather than a direct therapeutic claim.

    Public-assay heterogeneity caveat: pChEMBL values aggregate enzyme, cell, and functional readouts across labs. Apparent potency differences do not substitute for apples-to-apples preclinical characterization.
    Pipeline funnel

    51,711 genes in → 15 compounds out.

    Each transition corresponds to one explicit filter. Hover any flow to see the rule that narrowed the pool. No manual curation was applied; every edge is reproducible from the published code and intermediate tables.

    Funnel widths are proportional to gene / target / compound counts from the current build. The "No ChEMBL match" branch captures three novel targets with high replication confidence but no public chemical matter yet.
    What this is not
    • This is computational triage. No compound shown here has been characterized against thyroid-cancer cells, tissues, or animal models by the authors. Reported activities originate from heterogeneous public ChEMBL assays collected for unrelated indications.
    • Binding poses and pocket residues are reference views derived from public PDB entries (co-crystal or apo) or from AlphaFold-predicted models. No docking, molecular dynamics, or free-energy calculation has been performed to justify the compound overlays.
    • Replication-validated biomarkers are statistical signals in transcriptomic data, not validated therapeutic vulnerabilities. Driver status, dependency, and tumor-type specificity all require orthogonal experimental work.
    • Nothing on this page is a drug-development claim, a clinical recommendation, or an investment solicitation. It is a research visualization layered on public data.
