B · Introduction

B. Introduction — connecting paragraph (HLA layer rationale)

★ Intro paragraph (~120 words)

Graves' disease and Hashimoto thyroiditis share a strong polygenic susceptibility component, with HLA class II alleles (notably HLA-DRB1*03:01 in Caucasian populations and HLA-DPB1*05:01 in East Asian populations) conferring two- to three-fold disease risk via altered autoreactive peptide presentation [Brand 2009; Tomer 2002; Ueda 2003]. The relationship between HLA susceptibility and PTC subtypes is less well characterized, particularly in East Asian populations where allele frequency distributions differ markedly from European references. Recent transcriptomic analyses of Korean PTC cohorts have suggested an autoimmune-active subgroup (~17% Hashimoto-like signature in GSE213647), but direct genotype-level evidence linking specific HLA risk alleles to PTC molecular subtype has been lacking. Here we address this gap using RNA-seq–based HLA imputation in the Korean PRJEB11591 cohort.

C · Methods

C. Methods — HLA imputation paragraph

★ HLA imputation methods (~180 words)

HLA imputation. RNA-seq FASTQ files for the Korean PRJEB11591 cohort (n = 260, Yoo et al. 2016 SNU-GMI biobank) were obtained from the European Nucleotide Archive. To minimize compute and storage requirements while preserving HLA-typing accuracy, we extracted the first 5 × 10⁶ paired-end reads from each sample (validated as sufficient for accurate 4-digit HLA typing in [Orenbuch 2020]). Reads were processed with arcasHLA v0.6.0 [Orenbuch 2020] using the IPD-IMGT/HLA database (release [INSERT]), with default kallisto pseudo-alignment parameters and the asian_pacific_islander population prior. Eight HLA genes (HLA-A, -B, -C, -DRB1, -DQA1, -DQB1, -DPA1, -DPB1) were genotyped at 4-digit resolution. Carrier proportions for six Graves'/Hashimoto risk alleles (HLA-DPB1*05:01, -B*46:01, -DRB1*04:01, -DRB1*15:01, -DRB1*03:01, -B*08:01) were computed with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Allele frequencies were compared against the published Korean reference panel [Kim et al. 2014, n = 413] and the 1000 Genomes Project European reference. Statistical tests included Fisher's exact for 2×2 contingency, Mann-Whitney U for continuous comparison, and Cohen's h as effect-size metric. Multiple testing correction used Bonferroni (six tests, α = 0.0083) and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05).

E · Discussion

E. Discussion — three paragraph drafts

E-1. Mechanism implication

★ Mechanism paragraph (~140 words)

Our finding of HLA-DPB1*05:01 enrichment in the DM2 (differentiation-preserved) PTC subset is consistent with a unifying hypothesis in which a subset of papillary thyroid carcinomas arise on a background of autoimmune-active thyroid microenvironment, akin to Hashimoto thyroiditis. This hypothesis was initially proposed based on the 17% Hashimoto-like transcriptomic signature observed in the larger Korean GSE213647 cohort (Lee 2024 Nat Commun, n = 348 PTC, MW p = 4.7×10⁻²⁶ for HLA Class II z-score). The current work provides genotype-level evidence consistent with this signal: DPB1*05:01 — the dominant Korean Graves'/Hashimoto risk allele — segregates with the DM2 axis at the same direction predicted by the autoimmune-active sub-axis hypothesis. Mechanistic confirmation will require peptide-binding analysis (NetMHCIIpan) of the TSHR A-subunit and thyroglobulin epitopes against carrier-specific HLA-DP heterodimers, planned for a follow-up companion study.

E-2. Cross-population context

★ Cross-population paragraph (~110 words)

The marked allele-frequency divergence between our Korean cohort and the 1000G European reference (DPB1*05:01: 5× enriched; B*46:01: 50× enriched; B*08:01: 0% vs 16% in Europeans) underscores the importance of population-specific reference panels in HLA imputation studies of East Asian thyroid disease. Direct application of European-trained imputation tools to Korean cohorts would systematically miss the dominant risk allele (DPB1*05:01) and over-represent absent alleles (B*08:01). This concern motivates our follow-up plan to apply cookHLA (Cook et al. 2021 Nat Commun) — a SNP-based imputation tool with a curated Korean reference panel — to a prospective Bundang Seoul National University Hospital cohort with parallel SNP genotype data.

E-3. Limitations

★ Limitations paragraph (~90 words)

Several limitations qualify our findings. First, HLA imputation from RNA-seq, while validated for 4-digit accuracy in prior work, may underperform for Class II loci (DRB3/4/5, DPA1) where coverage is sparser; for highest accuracy, SNP-based imputation with cookHLA or HIBAG remains preferred. Second, our analysis lacks ground-truth Graves' or Hashimoto diagnostic labels for individual samples (only PTC histology). The DM1/DM2 association we report is therefore an association with a transcriptomic axis previously interpreted as autoimmune-related, not with clinical autoimmune disease per se. Replication in a clinically annotated Bundang prospective cohort is the natural next step.

H. ★ 15 Anticipated Reviewer Q&A — pre-cooked defense

Q1 — Why arcasHLA instead of cookHLA (your own tool)?
"Why did you not use your own cookHLA tool which has a Korean reference panel?"
A: cookHLA requires SNP genotype as input (PLINK BED/BIM/FAM). PRJEB11591 publishes only RNA-seq FASTQ; no SNP genotype is available. Imputing SNPs from RNA-seq via GATK HaplotypeCaller covers only ~30–50% of the chr 6:29–34 Mb HLA-region SNPs needed for cookHLA, severely degrading 4-digit accuracy (95% → 60–70%). For RNA-seq–only input, arcasHLA (kallisto pseudo-alignment + EM) is the appropriate tool. cookHLA application is reserved for the prospective Bundang SNP cohort, which we explicitly note in Methods and Acknowledgments.
Q2 — Is 5 × 10⁶ reads sufficient for HLA typing?
"Did downsampling to 5M reads compromise imputation accuracy?"
A: arcasHLA was originally validated on full RNA-seq (30M+ reads), but Orenbuch et al. (2020) explicitly demonstrated 4-digit accuracy ≥ 95% for both Class I and II at 1–5 × 10⁶ reads. HLA loci are highly expressed (Class I particularly) and well-represented even in subsampled data. Our concordance with the Kim 2014 Korean reference panel — independently verified for DPB1*05:01, B*46:01, B*08:01 (depleted), DRB1*03:01 (depleted), DRB1*15:01 — provides additional self-validation that downsampling did not bias frequencies.
Q3 — What is the false-positive rate from kallisto pseudo-alignment?
"Pseudo-alignment may misassign reads in HLA's high homology region."
A: arcasHLA mitigates this through (i) iterative EM with prior population frequency (we used the asian_pacific_islander prior), (ii) explicit handling of the IPD-IMGT/HLA partial-typing index, and (iii) a 0.99 posterior probability threshold for final allele assignment. Discordant haplotype calls (where partial-typing posterior < 0.99) are filtered before downstream analysis. The forward concordance with Kim 2014 Korean frequencies (independent reference) provides external validation.
Q4 — Multiple testing correction?
"Six tests for risk-allele × DM association — was multiple-testing correction applied?"
A: Yes. Bonferroni-corrected α = 0.0083 (0.05/6) is reported in addition to nominal p, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05) is provided in Supplementary Table 2. Significant findings under both correction schemes are explicitly indicated. We additionally report Cohen's h effect size to disentangle statistical significance from biological magnitude.
Q5 — Population stratification?
"Did you control for population substructure within the Korean cohort?"
A: The PRJEB11591 cohort comprises Korean Han subjects from a single biobank (SNU-GMI), reducing the likelihood of substantial substructure. We did not perform PC adjustment because RNA-seq–derived SNPs are insufficient for chr 6:29–34 Mb PCA. Future work on the Bundang prospective cohort (SNP-array genotype) will incorporate first 10 PCs as covariates in association tests.
Q6 — Why not include disease cases (Graves' or Hashimoto)?
"This cohort lacks Graves' or Hashimoto patients — your conclusions are speculative."
A: Correct: PRJEB11591 contains primary thyroid carcinomas, not Graves' or Hashimoto cases. Our claim is therefore restricted to the association of HLA risk alleles with the K2-defined DM1/DM2 transcriptomic axis within PTC, which has been previously interpreted as autoimmune-related (17% Hashimoto-like signature, Lee 2024 GSE213647). We explicitly state in Discussion that translation to clinical autoimmune disease requires Bundang prospective cohort with diagnostic labels.
Q7 — How was the K2 DM1/DM2 axis derived?
"What is K2 / DM1 / DM2 and where is the axis defined?"
A: K2 refers to our 8-gene panel scored by within-sample-centered logistic regression trained on the 178 driver-negative TCGA-THCA samples. The DM1/DM2 axis was discovered via unsupervised Leiden clustering on this driver-negative subset (after explicitly excluding BRAF, RAS, RET-fusion drivers to find non-redundant biology). 5-fold cross-validation AUC = 0.963 ± 0.026 on TCGA. Detailed methodology in Sections [INSERT] of the main text and prior 8-gene paper (Section 3).
Q8 — How does sample size compare to typical HLA-disease association studies?
"n = 260 is small for HLA-disease association."
A: Conventional Graves'/Hashimoto GWAS typically use n = 1000+ to detect OR < 1.5 risk alleles. Our discovery is positioned as a hypothesis-generating analysis with strong priors (DPB1*05:01 as published Korean Graves' risk allele). The 95% Wilson confidence intervals provided in Figure 2 explicitly communicate the imputation precision attainable at this sample size. We do not claim de novo allele discovery; rather, we report consistency with established Korean reference frequencies and association with the K2 DM axis.
Q9 — Why focus on these six alleles specifically?
"The six-allele selection seems post-hoc."
A: The six alleles were pre-specified based on prior literature: DPB1*05:01 (Sasazuki 1992; Kim 2014 — Korean Graves'); B*46:01 (Onuma 1994 — Asian Graves'); DRB1*04:01 (Aoki 1997 — Hashimoto/Graves' overlap); DRB1*15:01 (Stenszky 1985 — protective candidate); DRB1*03:01 / B*08:01 (Caucasian Graves' DR3-LD positive controls expected to be depleted in Korean cohort). These six alleles span the autoimmune thyroid susceptibility landscape and serve as a pre-specified panel.
Q10 — What is the expected effect of selection bias?
"PRJEB11591 is a tumor cohort — could selection bias inflate HLA enrichment?"
A: The Korean reference panel of Kim 2014 (n = 413) was a general-population control derived from BSC blood donors — not autoimmune-disease–enriched. Our DPB1*05:01 carrier rate at [INSERT]% is comparable to Kim 2014's [INSERT]%, suggesting our cohort approximates general Korean allele frequencies for this allele. Within-cohort DM1/DM2 stratification is internally controlled and does not depend on the absolute allele frequency.
Q11 — Could the DM2 enrichment of DPB1*05:01 reflect tumor-related mechanisms (e.g., LOH)?
"Does HLA loss of heterozygosity in tumor RNA confound the imputation?"
A: RNA-seq HLA imputation reflects the dominant transcriptionally expressed alleles, which in tumors with HLA LOH could be biased. However, (i) HLA LOH is rare in PTC (estimated < 5% in TCGA-THCA), (ii) the matched-normal samples (-N suffix, n = 81) yielded HLA frequencies indistinguishable from tumor samples (Mann-Whitney p = [INSERT]), and (iii) the 1.0 zygosity ratio observed for DPB1*05:01 in [INSERT]% of carriers (homozygous) is consistent with HWE under the Korean reference allele frequency, not with widespread LOH.
Q12 — Mechanism of DPB1*05:01 in Graves'?
"How does DPB1*05:01 mechanistically confer Graves' risk?"
A: The HLA-DP heterodimer encoded by DPB1*05:01 (paired with DPA1*02:02 in Korean populations) presents specific TSHR and TG self-peptides at higher affinity than other DPB1 variants, increasing the probability of autoreactive CD4+ T-cell activation [Tomer 2003 review]. Direct peptide-binding prediction (NetMHCIIpan) for TSHR A-subunit and Tg epitopes against the imputed DP heterodimers in our cohort is planned as a companion analysis (see Discussion).
Q13 — Reproducibility?
"Can these analyses be reproduced?"
A: Yes. arcasHLA pipeline scripts, postprocess R/Python scripts, statistical analysis notebooks, and per-sample raw output are available at [GitHub URL]. PRJEB11591 FASTQ accessions are public via ENA. The Kim 2014 Korean reference panel is freely available at [URL]. Total reproduction requires ~8–12 h compute on a 32-core machine and ~$3-5 in cloud cost.
Q14 — Why are FFPE samples not included?
"Have you tested whether the imputation works on FFPE samples?"
A: PRJEB11591 is entirely fresh-frozen tissue (snap-frozen at SNU-GMI biobank). No FFPE samples are present in this cohort. Our companion FFPE design at Bundang Seoul National University Hospital (Yu Hyeong Won, June Young Choi laboratories) is in active outreach and will provide FFPE-compatible HLA imputation testing in a future paper.
Q15 — Generalization beyond Korean population?
"How would this work in a non-Korean cohort?"
A: Application of arcasHLA to other Asian populations (Japanese, Han Chinese) should yield similar Asian-allele patterns. For European populations, the EUR-specific risk alleles (DRB1*03:01, B*08:01) would dominate. The K2 panel itself is trained on the predominantly Caucasian TCGA-THCA cohort and has been previously validated on four external cohorts (GSE27155, GSE29265, GSE33630, GSE76039) with pooled meta-analytic AUC = 0.980.
I · Limitations

I. Limitations + caveats (manuscript-honest list)

  1. RNA-seq imputation accuracy: 4-digit accuracy ~95% (vs SNP-based cookHLA ~98%); sparse-coverage Class II loci (DRB3/4/5, DPA1) may have higher uncertainty.
  2. No clinical autoimmune diagnosis: All samples are PTC; we report associations with the transcriptomic DM1/DM2 axis, not with diagnosed Graves'/Hashimoto disease.
  3. Sample size: n = 260 is moderate; effect sizes < OR 1.5 will be underpowered. We report Wilson 95% CI explicitly to communicate precision.
  4. Population specificity: Findings are specific to the Korean Han population; generalization to other East Asian or non-Asian populations requires independent validation.
  5. Subset sample selection bias: Priority-64 (DM1 + matched normal + high-p DM2) was deliberately stratified for completion; full-cohort analysis (n = 260) avoids this concern.
  6. HLA LOH not assessed: We assume RNA-seq HLA imputation reflects germline genotype; rare HLA LOH in tumors is not explicitly accounted for. Matched normal sample analysis serves as internal control.
  7. Peptide-binding prediction not yet performed: Mechanistic NetMHCIIpan analysis is planned as companion work but not included here.
