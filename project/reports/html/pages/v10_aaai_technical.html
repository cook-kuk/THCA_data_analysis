1. Theorem 1 phase transition (numerical)

Theorem 1 statement. Let ρ = ⟨w_Y, w_B⟩ be the cosine alignment between the label direction w_Y and the dominant batch direction w_B. If ρ² > ½, the trained classifier (under subtype-preserving ComBat + LODO) satisfies AUC_post < ½ as n→∞ — an actively wrong decision direction.

The panel below sweeps the cross-cohort label-direction cosine ρ_YY = ⟨e_Y_0, e_Y_1⟩ from −1 (platforms fully disagree) to +1 (platforms agree) in a realistic setup: p = 200, 40 samples/cohort, strong Y↔B confounding (85% vs 35% Y=1 rate), subtype-preserving ComBat via inmoose.pycombat_norm. The flip is sharp: mean AUC_post = 0.401 at ρ_YY ≈ −1 (classifier actively inverted), AUC_post = 0.791 at ρ_YY ≈ +1 (correct direction). The crossing through AUC = ½ occurs near ρ_YY ≈ −0.2, matching the extended flip condition (Eq. disc-full, K=2) in proofs/theorem1_proof.tex.

Full tabular data: results/v10_aaai/synthetic_benchmark/theorem1_numerical.tsv (21 ρ_YY bins × 20 replicates = 420 runs).

2. Synthetic phase diagram (σ_B × ρ_YY) — real ComBat-with-Y

Mean DIAL over a 4 950-run grid of (σ_B, σ_Y, ρ_YY, n) using inmoose.pycombat_norm subtype-preserving correction exactly as in v5.1. The platform-divergence parameter ρ_YY is the cross-cohort cosine of the label-direction vectors; ρ_YY < 0 means the two cohorts disagree on which genes move up/down with the subtype. Flip confirmed: mean AUC_post drops from 0.764 (ρ_YY > +0.4) to 0.339 (ρ_YY < −0.4), crossing ½ as the extended flip condition (Eq. 3 in proof) predicts.

Raw: results/v10_aaai/synthetic_benchmark/grid_results.tsv (4 950 rows). Predicted-vs-observed scatter: predicted_vs_observed_DIAL.png (Pearson r = 0.614).

3. Proposition 2 — quantum alignment probability

Proposition 2. For a uniform random quantum feature map φ_Q : ℝᵖ → ℋ with dim ℋ = 2^q, the flip-condition probability is bounded by Pr(A_Q > ½) ≤ 2^(−(2^q − 1)), i.e. doubly exponentially small in the number of qubits.

Numerical verification for q ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10, 12} with 1 000 random label/batch pairs each. Three probabilities are plotted on log scale: classical (blue), quantum random-unitary (red), and Qiskit ZZFeatureMap of depth 2 (purple); the dashed curve is the theoretical upper bound.

Raw: results/v10_aaai/quantum_bound/alignment_probability_vs_qubits.tsv.

3b. ZZFeatureMap 2-design Markov tail

Follow-up sweep: ZZFeatureMap(q, reps=r) at q ∈ {4, 6, 8} and r ∈ {2, 4} with up to 2 000 random-input pairs per cell. The 2-design prediction is E[A_Q] ≤ 2/d. Observed ratio E[A_Q] / (1/d) ranges from 1.19 to 1.54, within the bound. P(A_Q > ½) is empirically zero at q ≥ 6.

Raw: results/v10_aaai/quantum_bound/zz_markov_check.tsv.
