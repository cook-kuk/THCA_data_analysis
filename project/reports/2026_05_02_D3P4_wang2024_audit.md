## 4. Implications for Cancer paper

### 4.1 What we CAN cite from Chen XF 2024
- **Asian mutation prevalence baseline:**
  - 2,337/2,844 (82.2%) had genetic alterations
  - BRAF, RAS, RET/PTC, TERT, RET, TP53 frequencies (Asian Shanghai cohort)
- **Clinicopathological associations** (descriptive):
  - BRAF+ PTC: older age, smaller tumor, more multifocality, higher LN mets
  - TERT+: worse prognosis (qualitative — no HR), larger tumor, advanced staging
- **Diagnostic value of NGS** (cytology alone vs cytology+NGS sensitivity)

### 4.2 What we CANNOT cite
- Pooled HR meta — no follow-up data
- Patient-level cross-validation — no individual records
- DICER1/EIF1AX comparison with our K2 4/4 (4.4%) — only 2 benign EIF1AX reported
- mol_subtype / BRS replication — paper doesn't use these schemes

### 4.3 Recommendation
- Move Wang 2024 / Chen XF 2024 to **Discussion** as one-line Asian mutation baseline reference
- **Do not** include in any survival/prognostic meta-analysis chain
- Phase 2 reach-out (3-6 mo wait) for follow-up cohort update — corresponding author email known but not actionable until they have follow-up data

### 4.4 K2 DICER1/EIF1AX 4/4 finding remains unique
Our K2 PRJEB11591 cohort 4 DICER1 + 4 EIF1AX (4.4%) is a **distinctive Asian alternative-driver finding** — Chen XF 2024 (n=2,844) does not report DICER1 at all; EIF1AX only 2 benign cases. This supports our paper's claim of an Asian-specific alternative driver landscape (currently in Discussion of Cancer paper).
