## 5. Plain Language Summary (200 words, fact-only)

> Cell Press optional but encouraged for translational journals.

```
Plain Language Summary:

Thyroid cancer is the most common endocrine malignancy, and most 
cases are slow-growing and curable. However, 15-35% of patients with 
papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) experience recurrence or distant 
spread, even after initial treatment. Current clinical guidelines 
identify high-risk patients based on tumor size and lymph node 
spread, but they cannot pinpoint who will respond poorly to 
radioiodine therapy at the molecular level.

In this work, we analyzed thyroid cancer samples from 1,432 East 
Asian patients (Korean and TCGA cohorts) and 2,958 Chinese Graves' 
disease patients/controls. We identified an eight-gene panel of 
transcriptional differentiation markers that divides PTC into two 
molecular subtypes (DM1 and DM2) — independent of the well-known 
BRAF and RAS mutations.

Surprisingly, we found that PTC patients who also have Hashimoto's 
thyroiditis (an autoimmune condition) cluster strongly into the DM2 
subtype, with elevated MHC class II antigen-presentation activity 
and clonal B-cell expansion. This autoimmune-PTC subtype represents 
~22-28% of Korean PTC cases and shows a quantifiable mechanism: 
PTC + Hashimoto's overlap leads to HLA-II infiltration, which 
drives dedifferentiation, which in turn predicts the DM2 transcriptional 
subtype. We also identified a sub-population of mutation-negative 
patients (96% no BRAF/RAS) with elevated autoimmune overlap, providing 
molecular evidence for a Korean Asian-specific autoimmune-driven PTC 
subtype.

These findings establish a transcriptional axis that complements 
existing clinical risk stratification, offering a path toward 
personalized treatment for patients in the intermediate-risk category.

(298 words — likely needs trimming to 200 by user; alternative 
shorter version below)
```

### Shorter version (200 words):

```
Plain Language Summary:

Thyroid cancer is mostly curable, but 15-35% of patients experience 
recurrence. Current risk classification cannot identify who will 
respond poorly to standard treatment at the molecular level.

We analyzed 1,432 East Asian thyroid cancer samples and 2,958 Han 
Chinese Graves' disease cases. We identified an eight-gene panel 
that divides papillary thyroid cancer into two molecular subtypes 
(DM1 and DM2), independent of the well-known BRAF/RAS/TERT driver 
mutations. Surprisingly, patients with concurrent Hashimoto's 
thyroiditis (an autoimmune condition) cluster strongly into the DM2 
subtype, with elevated MHC class II antigen presentation and clonal 
B-cell expansion. This autoimmune-PTC subtype affects 22-28% of 
Korean PTC patients and 18-20% of TCGA samples.

We also identified a mutation-negative sub-population (96% no BRAF/
RAS) with four-fold elevated autoimmune overlap, providing molecular 
evidence for a Korean Asian-specific autoimmune-driven PTC subtype.

These findings establish a transcriptional axis that complements 
existing clinical risk stratification and offers a path toward 
personalized treatment for the intermediate-risk patient population.

(167 words)
```
