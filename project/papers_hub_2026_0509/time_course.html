★ Curation evolution patterns
- Pre-2018: small, manually-curated, positive-biased (McPAS, TSNAdb).
- 2019: aggregator era (IEDB pulls together 1000s of papers; mixed labels).
- 2020: patient-matched gold standards (TESLA, dbPepNeo HC).
- 2021: NEPdb's "tested but not recognized" inclusion philosophy — the OUTLIER that drives LOSO failure.
- 2023+: API-filtered (CEDAR), benchmark-format (IMPROVE), Zenodo-deposited (Neodb) — positive-class-biased again.
This evolution explains why a single uniform LOSO benchmark is mathematically hard: the corpus spans 8 years of changing curation philosophies.

Implications for benchmark design

  • Combining 2019 (IEDB, mixed) + 2021 (NEPdb, negative) + 2023 (CEDAR, positive) into one benchmark creates a non-stationary positive rate.
  • Strict LOSO is the right test because it forces the model to handle this curator-distribution shift.
  • Time-stratified analysis (train on 2017-2019, test on 2020-2021) would expose temporal drift but is out of scope here.
  • Future curators should adopt a standardized assay reporting + balanced positive/negative inclusion.

Honest caveats

  • The "publication year" assigned per source is the source's primary publication year, not per-record actual collection year.
  • Some records (e.g. IEDB) span decades of underlying experiments aggregated into a 2019 release.
  • The 2025 Neodb 100% positive rate is the curator filter, not actual biology.
