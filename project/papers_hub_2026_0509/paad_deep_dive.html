Lumenix · PAAD deep dive · 2026-05-08

Pancreatic Cancer (PAAD)
· Neoantigen Vaccine Re-analysis

Deep-dive re-analysis of the Lumenix neoantigen-vaccine corpus filtered to pancreatic cancer (PAAD). KRAS-driven (>90%), low-TMB ("dark cancer"), poor ICI response — but a small set of recurrent driver mutations make it the canonical neoantigen-vaccine target. We re-score KRAS G12 candidates, compare with COAD and LUAD, and audit HLA coverage for Korean and World populations.

1 · Why PAAD?

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PAAD) is one of the deadliest solid tumors (5-year survival ~12%). Three properties make it both attractive and hard for neoantigen-vaccine design:

  • >90% KRAS-mutant — among the highest-fidelity oncogene-driven cancers; offers a small set of recurrent peptide targets.
  • Low TMB (median ~3 mut/Mb) compared with melanoma (15) or lung (7.5) — limits non-driver neoantigen richness; ICI response baseline is poor (PD-1/PD-L1 mAb monotherapy ORR <5%).
  • G12D dominance (~45% of PAAD KRAS) — this single mutation represents nearly half of the entire patient population. A G12D-targeted vaccine would, in principle, address ~40% of all PAAD patients.
Strategic claim: a single shared neoantigen vaccine targeting KRAS G12D could plausibly serve a much larger PAAD patient population than personalized neoantigen vaccines. The trade-off is HLA restriction — only patients with a G12D-presenting HLA allele benefit.

4 · External corroboration in VDJdb

Among 134,633 records in VDJdb (171 chunks, full integration done in Phase KK), 290 cancer-positive peptides from our corpus have an exact match — independent TCR-binding annotation.

For our top KRAS G12D candidate GADGVGKSAL: a Lev-distance-1 neighbor GAAGVGKSAL (KRAS G12A variant) is in VDJdb with the species annotation HomoSapiens — strong descriptive support that a TCR exists for this peptide family.

5 · HLA presentation coverage

Figure 3 · HLA-A*11:01 + HLA-A*03:01 coverage by population. KRAS G12D peptides are HLA-A*11:01-restricted (Wells 2020 Cell, demonstrated in TESLA), with secondary A*03:01 binding.

★ The Korean problem: HLA-A*11:01 + HLA-A*03:01 cover only 9% of South Korean population vs 19.5% of World and 14.5% of US Caucasian. The dominant Korean HLA is A*24:02 (18.5% allele frequency) — and KRAS G12D peptides do not bind A*24:02 well. For Korean PAAD patients, we need either alternative G12D peptides that bind A*24:02, or different cancer drivers.

The same KRAS G12D mutation that gives top vaccine score in our XGBoost prioritization is the one with worst PAAD survival — this is the strongest possible biological rationale for prioritizing G12D as the vaccine target.

7 · ICI vulnerability framing — synthetic lethality

PAAD is one of the canonical "ICI-resistant" cancers (low TMB, immune-excluded TME). The standard immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA-4 mAb monotherapy) has <5% ORR in pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Our work suggests two synergistic angles:

  1. Neoantigen-vaccine + ICI combo — a KRAS G12D vaccine could create the priming step that ICI alone fails to do (no antigen-experienced T-cells to release). Combo trials (NCT04117087 etc.) are already exploring this.
  2. Synthetic lethality with ICI — KRAS G12D mutation creates a tumor-specific dependency that, combined with vaccine-induced T-cell expansion, could turn cold tumors hot. This is a 2-strike strategy.

9 · Honest limitations

  • Computational prioritization only; no wet-lab data on this specific candidate.
  • HLA-A*11:01 covers only 9% of Korean patients; A*24:02-restricted G12D peptides not in our top set — Korean cohort needs separate analysis.
  • The 0.871 RF score from broader-corpus training drops to ~0.69 under strict leakage-free training — keep both numbers visible.
  • External VDJdb corroboration is for KRAS G12A (GAAGVGKSAL), not G12D directly — descriptive support, not validation.
