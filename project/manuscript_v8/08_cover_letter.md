**Paragraph 1 (3-4 lines, hook + venue fit):**
We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "**An 8-gene panel reveals fusion-driven, epigenetically silenced dark matter in BRAF/RAS-negative thyroid cancer,**" for consideration as a research article in *Cell Reports Medicine*. Our work introduces an 8-gene RAI-responsiveness panel that identifies — within the BRAF/RAS-negative compartment of papillary thyroid carcinoma (~23% of cases) — a fusion-driven, epigenetically silenced subtype (DM1) that captures 81.8% of TCGA RET-fusion-positive tumors and supports a directly actionable reflex testing algorithm. We believe this combination of mechanism (a three-layer pathology spanning genetic, phenotypic, and epigenetic axes) and clinical translation (a reflex algorithm with a quantified upstream candidate pool of approximately 48 selpercatinib-eligible cases per 1000 incident PTC) aligns precisely with *Cell Reports Medicine*'s focus on translational discovery with immediate clinical implications.

**Paragraph 2 (5-6 lines, why important):**
Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common endocrine malignancy and one of the fastest-rising in incidence over the past three decades. Despite a >98% 5-year overall survival, structural disease recurrence reaches ~20% in ATA 2015 intermediate-risk patients (Haugen et al., 2016), and BRAF/RAS-negative tumors complicate radioiodine treatment decisions in the absence of mechanistic sub-stratification. The 2015 ATA Management Guidelines — recently updated as ATA 2025 (Ringel et al., 2025) — incorporate BRAF V600E as the sole molecular risk modifier; fusion drivers (RET/NTRK/ALK) and epigenetic silencing remain unaddressed in current risk stratification. Our work directly addresses this gap by providing an orthogonal molecular axis grounded in canonical RAI uptake biology (Yoo et al., 2016 *PLOS Genet*).

**Paragraph 3 (5-6 lines, what's new):**
The novelty of our framework lies in the integration of three mechanistic layers within a single clinically deployable panel. First, DM1 captures 76.8% of tyrosine kinase fusion-positive tumors (RET, NTRK, ALK, BRAF) at a molecular cluster level (OR 7.41 vs DM2). Second, DM1 fusion-positive and fusion-negative sub-clusters differ sharply in clinical and immune phenotype, defining a sub-B (fusion-negative, immune-overlap) population for which Paper 2 (in preparation) characterizes the autoimmune-PTC mechanism axis. Third, DM1 tumors — regardless of fusion status — exhibit fusion-independent promoter hypermethylation of canonical thyroid differentiation genes (TPO Cohen's d = 2.30; mean 8-gene β 0.385 vs 0.253 in DM2), providing a mechanistic rationale for hypomethylating-agent + radioiodine re-induction strategies. Importantly, our 8-gene panel was independently selected from canonical RAI biology and predates exposure to the Landa et al. (2016) advanced-disease ATC-silenced gene list — the 5-of-8 gene overlap with that list represents convergent biological validation across two independent panel designs, not panel-driven circular inference.

**Paragraph 4 (3-4 lines, generalizability):**
Our framework is supported by 4,300+ East Asian PTC tumors spanning four cohorts (TCGA, MSK-IMPACT, K2 / PRJEB11591, Lee / GSE213647, GSE286332) plus advanced-disease cross-validation (Landa et al., 2016) and external single-cell datasets (Pu et al., 2021 Fudan PTC; Lu 2023). The DM1 axis replicates across ancestries, reproduces in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue (Kolmogorov-Smirnov p = 0.44 between FF and FFPE), and confirms thyrocyte-intrinsic origin through patient-matched single-cell analysis (per-patient Spearman r 0.798–0.886). The pooled overall-survival hazard for DM1 in TCGA + MSK meta-analysis is 2.53 (95% CI 1.31–4.89; Cochran I² = 0%), quantifying clinical aggressiveness within Xing 2014 dark matter at a single-axis molecular level.
