# Voice-protected author sections

Claude may organize evidence and polish author-supplied language, but must not independently invent the final voice for these sections.

## V1 — Introduction opening hook

**Purpose:** describe the clinical paradox: excellent population-level survival alongside a subgroup exposed to repeated high-dose RAI with limited benefit and cumulative burden.

**Author input required:**

- What clinical situation feels most unacceptable or wasteful?
- What should the reader feel in the first three sentences: urgency, unfairness, preventable harm, or diagnostic uncertainty?
- Which toxicity or burden may be mentioned without overclaiming?

## V2 — Introduction final aim paragraph

**Factual ingredients:** TCGA discovery, DM1/DM2 lineage axis, driver/fusion/methylation analyses, external concordance, BRAF-context PFI stratification, post-RAI-refractory alignment, and harm-avoidance motivation.

**Author decision required:** which contribution is primary: biological state discovery, outcome stratification, or translational assay hypothesis?

## V3 — Discussion mechanism interpretation

Must connect cautiously to Landa 2016 and MAPK-associated dedifferentiation while distinguishing correlation from causation. Correct any citation misattribution to Krishnamoorthy 2025 if present.

**Author input required:** what mechanistic model does the author personally consider most plausible, and what evidence would falsify it?

## V4 — Limitations

Must disclose:

- retrospective public cohorts;
- low TCGA event rate;
- advanced-case enrichment in MSK;
- methylation correlation, not causality;
- small fusion-negative DM1 subgroup if confirmed;
- calibration or platform mismatch where applicable;
- non-confirmatory spatial analysis if retained;
- no completed internal IHC or RNA validation;
- no direct pretreatment RAI uptake/response measurement.

**Author input required:** which limitation most changes clinical interpretation, and what is the first realistic next validation study?

## V5 — Cover letter opening paragraph

Must explain why the work matters to Nature Communications readers without repeating the abstract or implying clinical readiness.

**Author input required:** why this paper should exist now, and why this journal rather than a thyroid-specific journal?

## Interview protocol

1. Ask no more than three focused questions at once.
2. Accept Korean, English, fragments, or dictation.
3. Preserve the author's reasoning and emotional emphasis.
4. Offer one restrained polished version and one more direct version.
5. Show all substantive additions in a “Claude-added content” note.
6. Do not merge the text into the manuscript until the author approves it.
