★ Why preserve conflicts?
  • Curator disagreement is data — these 2,221 peptides span multiple assays/contexts/patients. Resolving them requires either (a) trust one curator over another (arbitrary), or (b) wet-lab re-validation in a unified setting.
  • Some are HLA-context-dependent — same peptide can be immunogenic in patient with HLA-A*02:01 but not in HLA-A*24:02 patient.
  • Some are assay-dependent — IFN-γ ELISpot positive but tetramer-binding negative is biologically informative (T-cell response without classical pMHC presentation).
  • Our cleaning pipeline preserves the conflict_flag column rather than collapsing — a reviewer can audit this.

★ The NEPdb negative-heavy effect: NEPdb has 98% negative labels (15,614/15,912), driven by its inclusion of "tested but not recognized" peptides. When a model trained on other sources is tested on NEPdb (LOSO), the predicted-positive rate doesn't match — this is why NEPdb-out LOSO AUROC drops to 0.40 with biophys features. ESM2-150M partially recovers (0.86) because PLM features are less affected by per-source label-distribution.

Implications for benchmarking

  • Cross-source benchmarks must report per-source LOSO breakdown (not just mean).
  • Models that look uniformly good across LOSO sources are likely memorizing source-distribution.
  • The 2,221 conflict peptides should be excluded from training/test or treated as ambiguous label.
  • Future benchmark papers should standardize on a curator-cleaned subset (e.g. drop conflict peptides + drop heavily-negative-imbalanced sources).
