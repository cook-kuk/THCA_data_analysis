TESLA benchmark in one slide

What the benchmark is, why it matters, and how we use it.
TESLA

Canonical public benchmark, but still too small to carry the whole claim.

The TESLA community dataset is the benchmark most papers point to when they want to compare neoantigen selectors. In the validation literature it is described as 5 subjects, 535 validated peptides, and 34 positives. That gives us a clean reference, but not a final answer.

why we care

It exposes generalization gaps.

TESLA is useful when we want to show that the model stack does not just score well on a private shortlist. It lets the page compare against prior work, but the real platform claim still depends on prospective data.

  • Use TESLA to show benchmark separation.
  • Do not use it as the only proof of victory.
  • Keep the locked batch as the final decision gate.

Why this is high impact

Short version: scale, honesty, utility, structure.
impact

This is not a toy scoreboard

It combines 13 sources, a leakage-audited benchmark, a nontrivial set-cover vaccine output, and structural evidence in a single page. The claim stops before wetlab proof, which makes the page safer, not weaker.

Why reviewers care

Because the comparison is explicit, the leak control is explicit, and the boundary is explicit. There is no hidden training overlap story left to discover later.

A new locked prospective batch with hit/fail labels would move this from research-grade selection to a validation story. That is the next real jump.
