EGFR Lung Cancer Driver Mutations

EGFR mutations drive ~15% of lung adenocarcinoma (Asian population: 30-50%). L858R (exon 21), exon 19 deletion (E746-A750), and the resistance mutation T790M (exon 20) are the dominant clinical targets. EGFR-mutant LUAD has TKI sensitivity but eventually develops resistance — neoantigen vaccine is the post-TKI rescue strategy.

Clinical context

  • L858R: most common EGFR sensitizing mutation; covers ~40% of EGFR-mutant LUAD. TKI-sensitive (osimertinib, gefitinib).
  • Exon 19 del: ~45% of EGFR-mutant LUAD. Slightly better TKI response than L858R.
  • T790M: most common acquired resistance mutation. ~50% of TKI-treated patients. Osimertinib targets this mutation specifically.
  • EGFR vaccine + osimertinib: combination strategy under exploration. Vaccine targets recurrent neoantigens to prevent escape.

Korean LUAD context

Korean lung cancer is enriched for EGFR mutations (~50% of LUAD vs ~15% Western). HLA-A*24:02 is the dominant Korean class I allele. EGFR L858R neoantigen prediction on HLA-A*24:02 specifically is a high-priority computational task for Paper 4 backlog.

★ Synthetic lethality combo: EGFR L858R/T790M peptide vaccine + osimertinib + anti-PD-1. The vaccine creates new T-cell epitopes; osimertinib kills bulk tumor; PD-1 blockade prevents T-cell exhaustion. Korean Asian patients have the highest EGFR-mutant fraction → maximum potential benefit.
