Paper 9 Perturbation Extension: Glutamine-Axis Vulnerability Validation Map

Perturbation roadmap for glutamine-axis vulnerabilities in lineage-silenced thyroid cancer.

Exploratory translational roadmap — not validated synthetic lethality

Executive Verdict

GO as a perturbation roadmap. The strongest allowed claim is that public-data dependency and expression evidence prioritize a testable glutamine-axis wet-lab queue in lineage-silenced thyroid cancer. The current order is SLC1A5 > GLUD1 > GLS. This is not a validated synthetic-lethality claim, not a clinical treatment recommendation, and not a patient-selection biomarker.

Allowed

Candidate prioritization, perturbation roadmap, wet-lab assay design, and falsifiable hypothesis generation.

Still weak

Thyroid-only dependency coverage is underpowered, and processed drug response coverage is sparse for SLC1A5/GLUD1.

Forbidden

Validated synthetic lethality, clinical recommendation, patient selection, and causal therapeutic target discovery.

Why Perturbation Matters

Correlation to intervention

Dependency correlations nominate candidates; perturbation tests whether the lineage-silenced state creates an actionable experimental vulnerability.

Wet-lab plan

The ranking becomes concrete: KD/KO, inhibitor where feasible, glutamine withdrawal, rescue, and lineage/metabolic readouts.

Falsifiability

The hypothesis fails if effects are nonspecific, not stronger in lineage-silenced models, or not rescued by glutamine/TCA pathway rescue.

Claim Boundary

Forbidden: validated synthetic lethality, clinical recommendation, patient selection, causal therapeutic claim, or drug-response-based treatment proposal. Local evidence supports prioritization only. Negative and weak evidence is retained: GLS BPTES concordance is weak/discordant, SLC1A5 and GLUD1 lack matched processed drug metadata, and strict thyroid CRISPR coverage is small.
